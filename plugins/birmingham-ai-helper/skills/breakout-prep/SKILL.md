---

Help the facilitator walk in ready. Breakouts are ~45 minutes, mixed experience levels, and the group talks more than the facilitator does.

## 1. Pin down the basics

Ask, in one message, for whatever isn't already clear:

- Topic and the one thing people should leave with
- Length and expected headcount
- Room setup — projector, whiteboard, laptops open or closed
- Audience skew: mostly engineers, mixed with PM/design, or students

Don't stall on missing answers. Pick sensible defaults, say what you assumed, and keep going.

## 2. Draft a run-of-show

Produce a timed agenda that leaves at least half the clock for the group:

- **Opening (5 min)** — one framing question everyone answers in a sentence, so every voice is in the room early
- **Setup (5-10 min)** — the smallest bit of context needed; a live demo beats slides
- **Core (20-25 min)** — 3-4 discussion prompts or a hands-on exercise, ordered easiest to hardest
- **Close (5 min)** — go around for one takeaway each, plus where to continue the conversation

## 3. Write the prompts

Every discussion prompt should be answerable from the attendee's own work — "where has an AI tool actually cost you time?" rather than "is AI good for engineering?" Include two or three backup prompts for when the room goes quiet, and one concrete example the facilitator can open with to prime the pump.

## 4. Hand over a prep checklist

Close with a short list: what to test beforehand (demo, wifi, API keys, screen share), what to bring, and a one-line fallback for each thing that could fail live.

Deliver the agenda and prompts as plain markdown in the conversation unless the facilitator asks for a file or handout.
